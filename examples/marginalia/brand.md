# Marginalia

A worked example of a 0.3 `brand.md`. It is the upstream identity for two design
systems: [`website/DESIGN.md`](website/DESIGN.md) and
[`product/DESIGN.md`](product/DESIGN.md). Both express this brand and neither
looks like the other.

## Strategy

### Overview

Marginalia is a reading and annotation workspace for people whose job is to read
carefully: researchers, archivists, analysts, and graduate students.

It started as a set of scripts one researcher wrote because every tool she tried
treated a document as content to be skimmed rather than a text to be worked.

What it really does is preserve the relationship between a reader and a text over
time. Notes stay attached to the passage that provoked them, across versions,
across years, and across the dozens of documents where the same idea recurs.

The problem it solves is not storage. It is that reading work evaporates. The
insight arrives once, gets written in a margin or a separate app, and is
unfindable by the time it matters.

Before: a folder of PDFs, a notes app, and a memory of having read something
relevant. After: a body of work that accumulates, where every note knows what
provoked it.

Long-term ambition: to be the place a career's worth of reading lives.

### Audience

Primary audience: people who read professionally and are measured on what they
understand, not on how fast they got through it. Doctoral researchers, policy
analysts, archivists, litigation associates. They routinely hold a document open
for hours and return to it for years.

They already believe their own method works, because it does, roughly. They are
skeptical of anything that asks them to reorganize their material to suit a tool,
and of AI features that summarize what they are paid to read closely.

Secondary audience: research librarians and departmental administrators who
choose and pay for tools. They care about durability, export, and whether the
work survives the vendor.

Not for: people who want to get through a document faster. Speed-reading,
summarize-my-inbox, and study-hack audiences are a different product with
opposite values.

### Positioning

Category: a reading workspace for long-term textual work.

Not a PDF reader. Not a note-taking app. Not a reference manager. Not an AI
summarizer.

The market layers into three tiers. Readers (Preview, Acrobat) render documents
and forget them. Note tools (Obsidian, Notion) hold thinking but lose the link to
the source. Reference managers (Zotero, Mendeley) catalog citations but do not
care what you thought. Nothing owns the space between the passage and the note.

Structural differentials:
- Annotations anchor to text, not to page coordinates, so they survive
  re-pagination and new editions
- Every note carries its provenance permanently, with no manual linking step
- The corpus is the unit of work, not the document
- Plain-text export of everything, always, with no degradation
- No summarization features, as a stated position rather than a roadmap gap

Territory: close reading. Marginalia owns the idea that attention is the scarce
resource and the thing worth preserving.

### Personality

Archetype: The Archivist / The Patient Scholar.

Attributes: Care, Continuity, Precision, Restraint, Durability, Attention.

What it IS: Quiet. Exact. Durable. Respectful of expertise.

What it is NOT: Not clever. Not urgent. Not productivity-coded. Not a study hack.
Not impressed with itself.

### References & Anti-References

#### References

- **The New Yorker:** Borrow its editorial confidence and its assumption that the
  reader will stay for a long paragraph. Do not borrow its visual nostalgia, its
  illustration style, or its cartoons.
- **A university library reading room:** Borrow the calm, the generous surfaces,
  and the sense that the room exists to serve the work. Do not borrow the
  institutional signage or the wood-panel gravitas.
- **Linear:** Borrow the restraint and the information hierarchy. Do not borrow
  the dark gradients, the neon accents, or the software-category vocabulary.

#### Anti-References

- **Productivity SaaS:** Reject inflated headlines, gradient hero sections,
  dashboard-as-homepage, and any claim about "10x" anything.
- **AI-first reading tools:** Reject the promise that the reader can skip the
  reading. It is the opposite of what this brand is for.

### Promise

Core promise:
- Your notes stay attached to what provoked them.
- Your work outlives the tool.
- Nothing here reads for you.

Base message: Marginalia keeps the connection between a reader and a text intact
over years, so that reading accumulates instead of evaporating.

Synthesizing phrase: Marginalia exists so that careful reading compounds.

### Guardrails

Tone summary: Calm, exact, unhurried, respectful.

What the brand cannot be:
- A productivity brand
- A speed or efficiency brand
- An AI brand
- A student-hack brand
- Anything that implies the reader is behind

Litmus test: If it promises to save the reader time, it is wrong.

## Voice

### Identity

We are for people who read for a living. Not the ones who need to get through
more, the ones who need to understand better, and who will be held to that
understanding years from now.

We are not a faster way to read. We are not a summarizer. We do not think the
reading is the problem. The problem is that the thinking gets lost, and it gets
lost because almost nothing keeps a note tied to the sentence that caused it.

We build slowly, we export everything, and we assume you will still be using this
in a decade. That assumption shapes every decision we make.

Essence: We protect attention and everything it produces.

### Tagline & Slogans

Primary: **"Read closer."** Use it standing alone. It carries the whole position
and does not need a supporting line.

Alternatives:
- "Reading that compounds."
- "Notes that remember why."

Slogans by context:
- Homepage: "Read closer."
- Product empty state: "Nothing here yet. Start with a passage."
- Conference booth: "For people who read for a living."
- Pricing page: "Your work outlives the tool."
- Export dialog: "Everything, in plain text, always."

### Message Pillars

**Provenance**
- Every note knows what provoked it.
- The link between passage and thought is never manual and never lost.

**Durability**
- Your work outlives the tool.
- Plain text in, plain text out, no degradation.

**Attention**
- Attention is the scarce resource.
- We protect it rather than economize on it.

**Corpus**
- The body of work is the unit, not the document.
- The same idea across forty sources is one thread.

**Restraint**
- We ship less on purpose.
- Nothing here reads for you.

### Phrases

- "Notes that remember why."
- "Reading that compounds."
- "The margin is where the work happens."
- "Attention is the scarce resource."
- "Your work outlives the tool."

### Vocabulary

| Concept | We call it | Not |
|---|---|---|
| A user's whole body of material | corpus | library, collection, workspace |
| A note anchored to text | annotation | highlight, comment, markup |
| The anchored span of source text | passage | selection, excerpt, snippet |
| A saved cross-document thread | thread | tag, collection, folder |
| The reading surface | reader | viewer, canvas, editor |

Product and feature names are sentence case in prose: "the reader", "a thread".
Capitalize only at the start of a sentence.

### Tonal Rules

- Write in short declarative sentences.
- Assume expertise. Never explain the reader's own field to them.
- Prefer the concrete noun to the abstract one.
- Name the mechanism, not the benefit. "Annotations anchor to text" over
  "never lose your notes".
- Never promise speed.
- Never imply the reader is behind, unproductive, or missing out.
- No exclamation marks.
- No second-person imperatives in marketing copy. Invite, do not instruct.
- State positions plainly, including unpopular ones.
- When something is a limitation, say it is a limitation.
- Use "we" for the company and "you" for the reader, never "users".
- Numbers only when they are measured. No rounded-up claims.

Identity boundaries:
- We are not a productivity tool.
- We are not a summarizer.
- We are not a reference manager.
- We are not a study aid.
- We are not building an assistant.

| We Say | We Never Say |
|---|---|
| "Read closer" | "Read faster" |
| "Annotations anchor to text" | "Never lose a note again" |
| "Attention is the scarce resource" | "Boost your productivity" |
| "We do not read it for you" | "AI-powered insights" |
| "Your work outlives the tool" | "Future-proof your workflow" |
| "The corpus is the unit" | "All your content in one place" |

## Visual

### Core Colors

- **Ink** `#1B1A17` (mandatory). The brand's voice in visual form. Permanent,
  warm, and never pure black, because pure black reads as screen rather than page.
- **Vermilion** `#B8422E` (mandatory). One point of heat, and the only one. Its
  meaning comes entirely from its scarcity. A surface that uses it twice has
  already used it too much.
- **Limestone** `#F4F1EA` (optional). A warm ground for surfaces where pure white
  feels clinical.
- **Rule** `#B4AEA2` (optional). For hairlines and dividers only. Never for text.

Avoid: saturated blues in any form. Every tool in the adjacent categories owns
one, and the association is with software rather than with reading.

### Typefaces

- **Public Sans**, the brand voice, for all reading and interface text.
  Fallback: `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, Segoe UI, sans-serif`.
  Licensing: a modified version of Libre Franklin (SIL Open Font License 1.1);
  the GSA modifications are a work of the United States Government and not
  subject to copyright. Verified.
- **Space Grotesk**, for metadata only: timestamps, counts, citation keys,
  version markers. Fallback: `ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, monospace`.
  Licensing: SIL Open Font License 1.1. Verified.

Two families, and the split is semantic rather than decorative. Public Sans
carries anything a person wrote. Space Grotesk carries anything the system knows.

### Photography & Illustration

Mood: quiet, textural, unpeopled, worked-in, patient.

Subjects: paper and its surfaces, marginal writing, the edges of bound volumes,
desks mid-work with the person absent, shelving at rest.

Avoid: stock photography of any kind, people looking at screens, hands on
keyboards, open-plan offices, anything shot in a coffee shop, anything with a
visible expression of delight.

Illustration: only diagrams, and only where a diagram explains a mechanism.
Never decorative illustration, never characters, never abstract shapes used as
texture.

### Art Direction

Design keywords: Generous margins. Evident measure. One accent. Textural ground.
Hairline rules. Unhurried hierarchy. No ornament.

Direction statement: The identity should communicate a working surface for text,
not an application that happens to contain text. Every decision should make the
words easier to stay with for a long time.

Visual territory: **the reference room of a research library.** One ink on warm
paper. Wide margins that exist so the reader can write in them. Hairline rules
instead of boxes. A single red used for the one thing that matters on the page,
the way a librarian's stamp is the only color in a ledger. Light that is even and
unremarkable. Nothing that glows, nothing that floats, nothing that asks to be
noticed before the text does.

This territory is brand-wide. Each surface should narrow it into its own premise
rather than restate it. See
[`spec/design-md-integration.md`](../../spec/design-md-integration.md).

## Governance

### Naming

Features are named for the mechanism, in sentence case: "threads", "passages",
"anchored notes". Never named with a coined proper noun, never capitalized
mid-sentence, never suffixed with AI, Pro, or Plus.

Reserved: "corpus", "passage", "thread", "annotation" carry the definitions in
Voice > Vocabulary and are not reused for anything else.

Will not use: Smart, Magic, Instant, Turbo, Copilot, Assistant, or any name that
implies the software is doing the reading.

New feature names require review before they ship publicly.

### Claims

Approved:
- "Annotations anchor to text rather than page coordinates." Evidence: the
  anchoring implementation, documented in the engineering architecture notes.
- "Everything exports to plain text." Evidence: the export test suite covers
  every content type.

Requires review before use: any statement about how many researchers use the
product, any comparison naming a competitor, any statement about data residency.

Prohibited:
- Any accuracy or time-saving percentage. None has been measured.
- Any claim that reading is faster with Marginalia.
- Any claim of compliance or certification the company does not hold.

An agent may draft a candidate claim, but it must be labeled a draft until a
human approves it and attaches evidence.

### Accessibility Commitments

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA on every public surface and in the product.

Beyond the target: the reader must remain fully usable at 200% zoom without
horizontal scrolling, and every annotation action must be reachable from the
keyboard.

Exceptions are tracked in the accessibility register and each one carries an
owner and a date.

This is the commitment. The measurement of specific color pairs and text sizes
belongs to each `DESIGN.md`, where the linter checks it.
